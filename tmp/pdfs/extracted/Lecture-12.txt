1 Introduction: The Power of Conditional Information
In many real-world scenarios, the probability of an event is influenced by the occurrence
of another event. For example:
•Software Testing:The probability of a software bug being critical, given that it
was found in the core module.
•Medical Diagnosis:The probability of a patient having a disease, given a positive
test result.
•Network Security:The probability of a cyberattack being successful, given that
a vulnerability exists.
•Machine Learning:The probability of an email being spam, given it contains
certain keywords.
This is the essence ofconditional probability—updating our beliefs based on new
evidence.

2.1 Building Intuition with Urn Example
Example: Urn with Colored Balls
An urn contains 4 Red balls and 6 Blue balls. Two balls are drawn one after the other
without replacement. What is the probability that the second ball is Red, given that
the first ball was Red?
Solution:
1.Condition:First ball is Red (already occurred)
2.Reduced Sample Space:After removing one Red ball:
•Red balls remaining:4−1 = 3
•Blue balls remaining:6
•Total balls:3 + 6 = 9
3.Probability Calculation:
P(Second Red|First Red) = 3
9 = 1
3
The condition "First is Red" reduced the sample space from 10 balls to 9 balls, changing
the probability.

2.3 Example 1: Flight Departures and Arrivals
The probability that a regularly scheduled flight departs on time isP(D) = 0.83; the
probability that it arrives on time isP(A) = 0.82; and the probability that it departs
and arrives on time isP(D∩A) = 0.78. Find the probability that: (a) the plane arrives
on time, given that it departed on time. (b) the plane departed on time, given that it
has arrived on time.
Solution:(a)
P(A|D) = P(D∩A)
P(D) = 0.78
0.83 ≈0.94.
(b)
P(D|A) = P(D∩A)
P(A) = 0.78
0.82 ≈0.95.
Interpretation:Knowing that a flight departed on time increases the probability of an
on-time arrival to 94%. Conversely, knowing it arrived on time suggests a 95% chance it
departed on time.

2.4 Example 2: Textile Industry Quality Control
In a textile process,10%of strips fail the length test (L),5%fail the texture test (T),
and only0.8%fail both tests. If a strip fails the length test, what is the probability it is
texture defective?
Solution:
P(T|L) = P(T∩L)
P(L) = 0.008
0.10 = 0.08.
Interpretation:Given that a strip is length defective, there is an 8% chance it is also
texture defective. This is much more informative than the overall texture defect rate of
5%.

3.2 The Product Rule
For independent events, the probability that both occur is the product of their individual
probabilities:
P(A∩B) =P(A)·P(B).
For dependent events, we use the general product rule:
P(A∩B) =P(A)·P(B|A).

3.4 Example 4: Fire Engine and Ambulance Availability
A small town has a fire engine and an ambulance. The probability the fire engine is avail-
able is 0.98, and the ambulance is available is 0.92. Assuming they operate independently,
find the probability that both are available.
Solution:LetAbe the event the fire engine is available, andBbe the event the
ambulance is available.
P(A∩B) =P(A)·P(B) = 0.98×0.92 = 0.9016.
Interpretation:There is a 90.16% chance that both emergency vehicles are available
when needed.
