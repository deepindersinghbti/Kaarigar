probability of a correct diagnosis for a person with cancer is 0.78, and the probability of

probability that an adult is diagnosed as having cancer?
Solution:LetCbe the event of having cancer, andDbe the event of being diagnosed

P(C) = 0.05, P(D|C) = 0.78, P(D|C ′) = 0.06.

2 Introduction: The Power of Bayesian Updating
Bayes’ Theorem is one of the most important rules in probability theory. It provides
a way to update our beliefs when we observe new evidence. This is the foundation of
Bayesian inference, which is widely used in:
•Spam Filtering:Updating the probability that an email is spam based on its
content.
•Medical Diagnosis:Calculating the probability of a disease given a positive test
result.
•Machine Learning:Naïve Bayes classifiers used in text classification and senti-
ment analysis.
•Computer Vision:Object detection and recognition.
•Cryptography:Breaking codes by updating the probability of different keys.

3 Bayes’ Theorem
3.1 Concept and Visual Representation
Bayes’ theorem answers the question: "Given that eventAhas occurred, what is the
probability that it was due to a specific causeBi?"
S
B1 B2 B3
A
A∩B 1
A∩B 2
A∩B 3
Figure 4: Bayes’ Theorem, visually. We already knowAhappened (purple); Bayes’ rule
asks whatshareof that purple region sits inside each stripBr – that share is the updated
(posterior) probability ofBr.
P(B r |A) = P(B r)P(A|B r)
3X
i=1
P(B i)P(A|B i)
3.2 The Formula
For a partitionB1, B2, . . . , Bk of the sample space, Bayes’ rule states:
P(B r|A) = P(B r)·P(A|B r)Pk
i=1 P(B i)·P(A|B i)
, r= 1,2, . . . , k.
3.3 The Intuition
Bayes’ theorem is essentially a "reverse conditional probability":
•Prior Probability:P(B r)- our initial belief about the cause.
•Likelihood:P(A|B r)- probability of seeing evidenceAif causeB r is true.
•Evidence:P(A)- total probability of seeing the evidence.
•Posterior Probability:P(B r|A)- updated belief after seeing evidence.

5 Example 2: Determining the Most Likely Plan
A manufacturing firm employs three analytical plans for the design and development of
a particular product. Plans 1, 2, and 3 are used for30%,20%, and50%of the products,
respectively. The defect rates are:
P(D|P 1) = 0.01, P(D|P 2) = 0.03, P(D|P 3) = 0.02.
If a random product was observed and found to be defective, which plan was most likely
used?
Solution:First, calculate the total probability of a defect:
P(D) = (0.30)(0.01) + (0.20)(0.03) + (0.50)(0.02) = 0.003 + 0.006 + 0.010 = 0.019.
Now apply Bayes’ theorem:
P(P 1|D) = (0.30)(0.01)
0.019 = 0.003
0.019 ≈0.158.
P(P 2|D) = (0.20)(0.03)
0.019 = 0.006
0.019 ≈0.316.
P(P 3|D) = (0.50)(0.02)
0.019 = 0.010
0.019 ≈0.526.

8 Key Takeaways
Key Points for CSE Students:
•TheLaw of Total Probabilityhelps compute probabilities by considering all
possible scenarios.
•Bayes’ Theoremis the foundation of Bayesian inference, used in:
–Spam filtering (Naïve Bayes)
–Medical diagnosis
–Machine learning classification
–Computer vision
–Natural language processing
•The theorem allows us toupdate our beliefswhen new evidence is observed.
•Thepriorrepresents our initial belief, and theposteriorrepresents our up-
dated belief.
•Thelikelihoodis the probability of seeing the evidence given the cause.
•Bayes’ theorem is essentially a "reverse conditional probability" calculation.
•Tree diagrams are excellent tools for visualizing and computing Bayesian prob-
abilities.
