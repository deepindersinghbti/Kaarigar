1 Why We Need Measures of Dispersion
Central tendency measures (Mean, Median, Mode) alone are insufficient. Consider this:
The Problem with Only Averages
Two datasets representing API response times (in ms):
Dataset A:100, 101, 102, 103, 104 (Mean = 102)
Dataset B:50, 75, 102, 129, 154 (Mean = 102)
Statistic Dataset A Dataset B
Mean 102 102
Median 102 102
Mode No mode No mode
Question:Are these datasets similar?
Answer:No! Dataset A is consistent (100-104 ms). Dataset B is highly variable
(50-154 ms).
This is why we needmeasures of dispersion- they tell us how spread out the
data is.
1.1 Why Dispersion Matters in CSE
Real-World Importance
•Performance Monitoring:High variance means unreliable performance
•Capacity Planning:Need to account for variability in resource usage
•Quality Assurance:Low variance means consistent quality
•Anomaly Detection:Unusual values are outside normal spread
•Risk Assessment:Higher spread means higher risk
•Network Engineering:Jitter (variation in latency) affects QoS

2.1 1. Absolute Measures of Dispersion
Absolute measures are expressed in the same units as the original data. They
provide the actual spread of data.
Absolute Measures Include:
•Range- Maximum - Minimum
•Mean Deviation- Average of absolute deviations from mean
•Variance- Average of squared deviations from mean
•Standard Deviation- Square root of variance
•Quartile Deviation- (Q 3 - Q1)/2
2.2 2. Relative Measures of Dispersion
Relative measures are expressed as ratios or percentages. They allow comparison
between different datasets regardless of their units or magnitudes.
Relative Measures Include:
•Coefficient of Range- Range / (Max + Min)
•Coefficient of Mean Deviation- MD / Mean
•Coefficient of Variation (CV)- (SD / Mean)×100
•Coefficient of Quartile Deviation- (Q3 - Q1) / (Q3 + Q1)
Note: Relative measures will be covered in detail in Lecture 7.

3.3 Why Standard Deviation is Preferred over Mean Deviation
The Key Problem with Mean Deviation
Consider two datasets:
Dataset R:0, 10, 10, 10, 20
•Mean = 10
•Deviations: -10, 0, 0, 0, 10
•Absolute Deviations: 10, 0, 0, 0, 10
•Mean Deviation = 20/5 = 4.0
Dataset S:2, 6, 10, 14, 18
•Mean = 10
•Deviations: -8, -4, 0, 4, 8
•Absolute Deviations: 8, 4, 0, 4, 8
•Mean Deviation = 24/5 = 4.8
Mean Deviation says:Dataset S is more spread out (4.8 > 4.0)
Now let’s check Standard Deviation:
Dataset R:
σ2 = 100 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 100
5 = 40⇒σ= 6.32
6/13

--- PAGE 8 ---
Dataset S:
σ2 = 64 + 16 + 0 + 16 + 64
5 = 32⇒σ= 5.66
Dataset Mean Deviation Standard Deviation
R 4.0 6.32
S 4.8 5.66
Standard Deviation says:Dataset R is more spread out (6.32 > 5.66)
Why the Difference?
Mean Deviation treats all deviations equally. Standard Deviation gives MORE
WEIGHT to larger deviations by squaring them.
In Dataset R: Deviations are -10, 0, 0, 0, 10 (two extreme values) In Dataset S:
Deviations are -8, -4, 0, 4, 8 (moderate spread)
The correct answer:Dataset R is more spread out because it has two values (0
and 20) far from the mean.
3.3.1 Additional Reasons Why Standard Deviation is Preferred
Why Standard Deviation is Superior:
1.Gives More Weight to Large Deviations:Squaring amplifies the effect of
outliers
2.Mathematically Differentiable:Allows for calculus-based optimization
3.Algebraic Properties:Variances add, making it useful for combining datasets
4.Foundation for Statistics:Used in z-scores, confidence intervals, hypothesis
testing
5.Standardization:Allows comparison across different scales
6.Normal Distribution:Standard deviation has special meaning in normal
distribution (68-95-99.7 rule)

5 Summary: When to Use Which Measure
Use Range when:
•Need quick, rough estimate of spread
•Data has no outliers
•For quick comparison
Use Mean Deviation when:
•Need easy interpretation
•Data has moderate outliers
•All deviations are considered
Use Standard Deviation when:
•Need most reliable measure
•Data is roughly symmetric
•For further statistical analysis
•Comparing variability between datasets
•Need to detect extreme values
Use Quartile Deviation when:
•Data has outliers
•Need robust measure of spread
•For skewed distributions

7 Key Takeaways for CSE Students
•Absolute Measures of Dispersion:
–Range: Simple but sensitive to outliers
–Mean Deviation: Easy to interpret but not differentiable
–Variance: Squared units, good mathematical properties
–Standard Deviation: Most widely used; same units as data
–Quartile Deviation: Robust to outliers
•Why Standard Deviation over Mean Deviation:
–Gives more weight to extreme deviations
–Differentiable and has nice algebraic properties
–Foundation for advanced statistics
–Allows standardization (z-scores)
–Used in hypothesis testing and confidence intervals
•Algorithm Complexity:
–Range: O(N) time, O(1) memory
–Mean Deviation: O(N) time, O(1) memory
–Standard Deviation: O(N) time, O(1) memory
–Quartile Deviation: O(N log N) time (sorting required)
•Real-World Applications:
–Performance monitoring: Standard deviation of response times
–Anomaly detection: Values beyond 3 standard deviations
–Capacity planning: Use mean + 3 standard deviations
–Quality control: Keep variability low
Remember:
•Meanalone is not enough - always check spread!
•Standard deviationis the most commonly used measure of dispersion
•Standard Deviationis preferred over Mean Deviation because it gives
more weight to extreme deviations
•In performance monitoring: Low mean + low standard deviation = reliable
system
•UseQuartile Deviationwhen data has outliers
